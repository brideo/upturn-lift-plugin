# Frameworks for the Head of Product

Use each one for the question it answers, and say when its limits matter.

## Crossing the chasm: beachhead and whole product (Geoffrey Moore)
- **What it is:** a model of how buyers adopt a product. Early adopters buy the vision. The early majority (pragmatists) buy proven, complete solutions, recommended by peers like them. Most products stall in the gap between the two groups.
- **What to do:** pick one **beachhead** segment and win it completely before moving to the next. Deliver the **whole product**: the core plus setup, integrations, support, documentation, training and proof. Pragmatists judge the whole package, not the core technology.
- **Use it for:** choosing the first market, and listing what's missing before mainstream buyers will commit.

## Jobs to be done
- **What it is:** customers "hire" a product to make progress in a situation. Write each job as: "When <situation>, I want to <motivation>, so I can <outcome>."
- **Use it for:** positioning, messaging, and finding which competing solutions actually matter (including doing nothing and hiring an agency).
- **Get them from:** past behaviour in interviews, not opinions.

## Opportunity solution tree (Teresa Torres)
- **What it is:** a tree with the desired outcome at the top, customer opportunities (needs, pains, desires) below it, then solutions, then assumption tests.
- **How it's run:** discovery is continuous, with weekly customer touch points, carried out by the product trio: product manager, designer and tech lead.
- **Use it for:** stopping teams jumping from an outcome straight to a feature.

## RICE prioritisation
- **The formula:** Reach (people per quarter) × Impact (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3) × Confidence (%) ÷ Effort (person-weeks).
- **Use it for:** ranking the roadmap.
- **Watch for:** weak evidence. Say what each score rests on, and lower confidence when evidence is thin.

## Product-market fit survey (Sean Ellis)
- **The question:** "How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?"
- **The benchmark:** 40%+ "very disappointed" is a rule of thumb from comparing startups. It isn't proof.
- **How to run it:** segment the answers to find the group that loves the product, and build for them first.
- **Its limits:** it measures stated feelings. Read it alongside retention.

## Van Westendorp price sensitivity
- **The method:** four questions: too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive.
- **What you get:** an acceptable price range.
- **When to use it:** only with real survey responses, never guessed. Until then, price from the value delivered and the alternatives' prices, and test.

## North-star metric
- **What it is:** one metric that captures value delivered to customers (not revenue), plus three to five input metrics the team can move.
- **Example for Lift:** verified fixes per active site per month.

## Now / Next / Later
- **What it is:** a roadmap by horizon instead of by date.
- **Now:** what the current bets depend on.
- **Next:** what's likely once those bets are proven.
- **Later:** direction, kept deliberately loose.
